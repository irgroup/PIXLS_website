The PIXLS Project & Scope

Preprint servers have transformed scientific communication in the life sciences, enabling immediate dissemination of groundbreaking results. However, traditional digital library systems struggle to index the rich multimodality of preprints.

The Challenge: Extracting Multimodal Structures from Scientific Literature

Scientific literature in biomedicine is inherently multimodal: the most essential experimental data, dosage tables, statistical results, and molecular observations are embedded within tables, figures, and visual layouts. Standard full-text search engines treat PDFs as flat strings of text, completely losing structural context and rendering tables and figures invisible to retrieval algorithms.

Furthermore, evaluating retrieval systems on new tasks requires costly human relevance judgments. Test collections age quickly, failing to reflect modern user requirements such as complex question-answering and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

The PIXLS Solution: Multimodal AI & Open Science

Funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG) in the LIS program (Wissenschaftliche Literaturversorgungs- und Informationssysteme), PIXLS tackles both dimensions:

  • ✓ Automated Ingestion Pipeline: Continuous harvesting of 530,000+ preprints from bioRxiv, medRxiv, and 30+ OSF archives using pixls-preprints.
  • ✓ Vision-Language Parsing: Moving beyond brittle legacy rule-based tools to Docling and modern Vision-Language Models (dots.mocr) with reanimator-vlm.
  • ✓ Synthetic Relevance Benchmarks: Generating validated multimodal relevance labels using LLMs (UMBRELA framework) to revive legacy test collections (TREC-COVID+).
  • ✓ Preprint RAG Demonstrator & LIVIVO: The ZB MED team has engineered a usable RAG demonstrator connecting preprint intelligence with LIVIVO, while the CIR team at TH Köln focused on advancing deep multimodal information extraction.

Featured Toolkit

reanimator-vlm

A clean rebuild of REANIMATOR using vision-language models (such as dots.mocr) for document collection processing, structured multimodal element parsing (tables, figures, formulas, text), and domain-independent synthetic relevance assessment via the UMBRELA framework.

VLM-Powered Extraction High-precision optical recognition of tables, visual elements, and formulas.
Synthetic Judgments Automated, reproducible multi-grade relevance labeling for RAG evaluation.
PyPI Package → GitHub Repository Technical Docs
Python Quickstart 
from reanimator import (
    DocumentCollection,
    MultimodalParser,
    SyntheticAssessor
)

# 1. Load collection of preprint PDFs
collection = DocumentCollection.from_folder("./preprints")

# 2. Parse multimodal components with VLM
parser = MultimodalParser(model="dots.mocr")
parsed_docs = parser.parse(
    collection,
    extract_tables=True,
    link_contexts=True
)

# 3. Generate synthetic relevance for RAG
assessor = SyntheticAssessor(framework="umbrela")
judgments = assessor.evaluate_relevance(
    query="remdesivir clinical efficacy",
    elements=parsed_docs.get_tables_and_passages()
)

print(f"Revitalized {len(judgments)} labeled pairs.")

Revealing the Hidden Success Factors of Scientific Papers: Structural, Linguistic, and Multimodal Dynamics

Fabian Haak and Philipp Schaer

Poster presented at the Conference on Search Engines and Society (SEASON 2026), Cologne, Germany.

Empirical Sample Case for REANIMATOR Parsing:

Demonstrating the real-world utility of the REANIMATOR pipeline, this study extracted and analyzed full multimodal elements (figures, tables, references, linguistic properties) across scientific corpora to uncover determinants of paper success.

  • Visuals matter: Use figures, describe them thoroughly in captions and running text, and place an early visual on page 1 or 2.
  • Writing tone: Use active first-person "we" and avoid passive voice. Complex language is fine, but avoid intensifiers and unnecessary synonyms.
  • Section balance: Avoid overly detailed method sections; keep the focus firmly on results.
  • Surprisingly insignificant: Latinate nominalizations, table caption length, exclamation marks, and abstract sentence length showed negligible correlation with impact.

Empirical Sample Case March 2026

Poster at SEASON 2026: Revealing Hidden Success Factors of Scientific Papers with REANIMATOR

At Search Engines and Society (SEASON 2026) in Cologne, Fabian Haak and Philipp Schaer present an empirical study investigating structural, linguistic, and multimodal determinants of scholarly paper success—serving as a direct real-world demonstration of the REANIMATOR parsing toolkit.

By leveraging REANIMATOR's automated document extraction pipeline, the study parsed thousands of scientific full-text PDFs to extract tables, figures, captions, and contextually linked in-text references alongside textual linguistic metrics.

Core Empirical Takeaways:

  • Prioritize Visuals: Papers that feature figures and describe them thoroughly in both the caption and running text achieve higher visibility. Crucially, placing an early visual on page 1 or 2 correlates strongly with citation success.
  • Author Voice: Using an active first-person voice ("we") outperforms passive constructions. While complex domain language is well tolerated, authors should avoid empty intensifiers and synonyms.
  • Focus on Results: Overly lengthy, dense methodology sections often deter engagement; focusing on clear empirical outcomes yields higher scholarly traction.
  • Insignificant Elements: Factors such as Latinate nominalizations, table caption length, abstract sentence length, and punctuation marks (exclamations, parentheses) showed negligible statistical significance.

This work showcases how PIXLS multimodal parsing turns raw PDF archives into structured analytical corpora for meta-research and information retrieval.

Software Release January 2026

Introducing REANIMATOR-VLM: Rebuilding Document Extraction with Vision-Language Models

We announced the release of reanimator-vlm (v0.1.2) on PyPI. This clean rebuild transitions the REANIMATOR extraction core from traditional OCR heuristics to state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (dots.mocr) and Docling.

Extracting information from scientific PDFs has historically been hindered by brittle layout parsers and closed LLM dependencies. reanimator-vlm solves this by providing:

  • VLM Layout & Table Recognition: High-accuracy parsing of complex biomedical tables, sub-tables, formulas, and visual elements.
  • In-Text Contextualization: Automatic linking of table and figure mentions in the narrative text to their corresponding visual containers.
  • Synthetic Relevance Engine: Domain-independent relevance assessment based on the UMBRELA framework for evaluating RAG and retrieval benchmarks.

Install today via pip install reanimator-vlm or inspect the open-source code on GitHub.

Publication Accepted July 2025

REANIMATOR Accepted at ACM SIGIR 2025 in Padua, Italy

Our research paper "REANIMATOR: Reanimate Retrieval Test Collections with Extracted and Synthetic Resources" has been accepted for presentation at the 48th International ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval.

Retrieval test collections are fundamental for IR progress but are notoriously expensive to construct and difficult to generalize. In this paper, we demonstrate how REANIMATOR revives the established TREC-COVID collection into TREC-COVID+.

By extracting machine-readable tables and contextual full-text snippets, and using LLMs to generate calibrated synthetic relevance labels, we prove that table-aware multimodal retrieval substantially enhances answer precision in biomedical RAG applications.

To support open scientific infrastructure and reproducible data pipelines, ZB MED and TH Köln have released our acquisition and transformation repositories on Codeberg.

ISI 2027 • Hamburg

Information Extraction in Practice

Information extraction (IE) has transitioned from research prototypes into everyday practice across libraries, archives, and research data services. While LLMs and VLMs have dramatically lowered entry barriers, deploying robust pipelines in production raises vital questions about quality assurance, data provenance, computational cost, and long-term service sustainability.

Co-organized by leaders from TH Köln, ZB MED, ZBW, and GESIS, this half-day workshop brings together practitioners and researchers from the DFG LIS programme, Leibniz institutes, and NFDI consortia to seed a sustainable community of practice.
